# English dialogue

- Respond to the speaker's situation and emotional stakes with concrete, natural language. Avoid canned reassurance and an all-knowing account of their feelings.
- Prefer connected speech to successive slogans, while retaining brief replies, interruptions, and pauses that fit conversation.
- Use imagery only when it fits the speaker's voice and shared experience. Analogy should earn its place in the exchange.
- Reduce explanatory afterthoughts and repeated emotional labels. Let warmth arise from attention to what the other person actually said.
- Preserve each fictional speaker's knowledge and motives. In assistant conversation, express care without inventing personal experiences or intimacy.
