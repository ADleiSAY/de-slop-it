# English literary writing

- Favor flowing sentences with purposeful rhythm over uniformly clipped prose; keep short sentences when they carry tension, silence, or a change of pace.
- Write through the subject's senses, bodily experience, desires, and limited knowledge. Consider what the reader can feel without an explanatory narrator naming every emotion.
- Use metaphor when its image is apt, beautiful, and consistent with the scene and voice. Use explanatory analogy sparingly.
- Respect the established point of view, including an intentionally omniscient narrator; avoid casually attributing unobservable thoughts to every character.
- Reduce trailing emotional labels and afterthought explanations. Integrate necessary meaning into the sentence or action while preserving deliberate hesitation and associative rhythm.
