# English formal writing

Initial adaptation of the user's preferences; refine through explicit feedback.

- Develop the claim through reasons and evidence. Reduce habitual rebuttal framing while preserving meaningful distinctions.
- Prefer connected, well-shaped sentences over a procession of short declarations. Use subordination to clarify conditions and consequences; keep sentence boundaries where comprehension benefits.
- Use established, precise vocabulary. Avoid coined acronyms, translated shorthand, and compressed labels unfamiliar to the audience. Do not import Chinese idioms or archaic diction literally.
- Prefer concrete actions and supported examples to decorative metaphors and analogies. Connect each substantial point to the preceding argument and the central question.
- Make supported causal relations explicit with varied, appropriate constructions, such as because (e.g.), owing to (e.g.), consequently (e.g.), and through (e.g.). Preserve the distinction between association and causation.
- Use verified quotations where they add explanatory value. Quotation marks can identify quoted speech, a term under discussion, or an intentionally special sense; avoid gratuitous scare quotes.
- Let syntax and meaning guide punctuation. Semicolons can connect related independent clauses. Questions and exclamations require a real rhetorical purpose appropriate to the genre; avoid habitual colon and em-dash constructions.
